PROJECT DEEP DIVE

Phishing Triage & Incident Response Playbook

INCIDENT RESPONSE & THREAT ANALYSIS

Engineered a comprehensive incident response playbook and analysis workflow targeting credential compromise via spear-phishing attacks[cite: 3, 4]. The framework follows structured guidance from NIST SP 800-61 and maps directly to the MITRE ATT&CK framework (T1566 Phishing, T1078 Valid Accounts)[cite: 4, 6].

Incident Overview & Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)

Simulated a scenario where an employee received a phishing email impersonating a trusted service provider, leading to credential harvesting and unauthorized corporate account access[cite: 4]. Key IOCs identified include:

  • Suspicious Logins: Login attempts originating from unusual IP addresses or unfamiliar geographic locations[cite: 4].
  • Account Anomalies: Unauthorized email forwarding rules and multi-factor authentication (MFA) fatigue notifications[cite: 4].
  • Outbound Activity: Reports of suspicious or malicious emails transmitted from the compromised employee account[cite: 4].

Email Header & SPF Record Analysis

Conducted deep-dive header analysis to differentiate between benign vendor communications and malicious spoofed emails[cite: 9, 11]:

  • Authentication Headers: Evaluated Authentication-Results, DKIM passes, and SPF validation parameters across incoming mail servers[cite: 9, 11].
  • Domain Verification: Used domain tools (Dmarcly) to inspect and flatten SPF records, validating permitted sender IP blocks and detecting misconfigured policy rules[cite: 10, 12].
  • Attachment Triage: Established safe inspection protocols utilizing VirusTotal, full message source headers, and Windows Defender runtime scanning to isolate suspected malicious attachments[cite: 12].

Structured 5-Stage Incident Response Lifecycle

  • Phase 1 - Acquire & Preserve: Harvested original email headers, authentication logs, VPN records, and identity provider telemetry to establish timeline integrity[cite: 4, 5].
  • Phase 2 - Containment: Executed immediate containment by disabling compromised accounts, forcing credential resets, revoking active session tokens, and purging unauthorized inbox rules[cite: 5].
  • Phase 3 - Eradication: Rotated compromised passwords, enforced strict organization-wide MFA, and updated email security gateway filters to block malicious infrastructure[cite: 6].
  • Phase 4 - Recovery: Verified normal operations, monitored outbound traffic for residual anomalies, and provided targeted user support[cite: 7].
  • Phase 5 - Lessons Learned: Documented root causes (insufficient phishing awareness, weak credential controls) and recommended defensive upgrades such as conditional access policies and recurring simulations[cite: 8].
